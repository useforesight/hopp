Hopp for Omarchy · native Rust + GPUI

Pairing that fits a tiling desktop, not one big window.

The Linux client used to be a single 1280×820 window that Hyprland tiled like anything else, so the shared screen got letterboxed into odd shapes and cameras stretched with the layout. Now every part of a call is a small surface shaped for what it shows, and Hopp itself lives in the Omarchy bar.

01 · The idea

A tile has the compositor’s shape. A screen has its own.

Fitting a fixed-shape screen into an arbitrary tile always leaves bars, and floating it opts out of tiling. So the client stops treating a tile as a picture of the remote screen and treats it as a window onto it.

Before

One window does everything

  • Sidebar, stage, filmstrip and settings all in one tile.
  • Shared screen letterboxed into whatever shape the tile is.
  • Camera cards stretch and shrink with the layout.
  • Closing the window ends the session.
Now

Surfaces shaped for their content

  • The bar is the app: teammates, rooms, call controls.
  • Faces live in a small floating window that grows one square tile per person.
  • The shared screen is an ordinary tile that crops to a readable scale and follows the sharer.
  • The whole screen is one key away in a scratchpad workspace.
  • The call lives in a shared session model; windows come and go freely.

02 · Bar app & Hopp window

Hopp lives in the bar

The local.hopp shell plugin shows the Hopp mark when idle and a live pill during a call: mini avatars, the timer, your mic state and a red dot while you share. Its panel is keyboard-driven — type to search, ↑↓ to choose, ⏎ to pair. It starts the client in the background when the shell starts.

The Hopp window below is the same content as a small floating window: it’s where you sign in and change settings, and it covers everything if the bar plugin isn’t installed.

Pair hovers in. Rows stay quiet until you point at them; then Pair (or Invite, mid-call) and Join appear. Presence shows where someone is: Available, Away, or the room they’re in.
03 · Call window

Faces never stretch

The call window’s size is derived from the number of people, not chosen by dragging: one square tile per person plus the controls. Cameras are centre-cropped to fill their square, so a 4:3 or 16:9 webcam never distorts.

  • Three tile sizes — S, M, L — and three layouts: column, row and round bubbles. Hover the window to switch; it remembers.
  • The sharer leads, you’re last. Camera off shows initials; badges mark mute, sharing and control.
  • Floating and pinned to every workspace, never steals focus, and excluded from your own screen share.
  • Hyprland ignores a floating window resizing itself, so Hopp asks Hyprland directly and keeps the window pinned to its nearest corner as people join.
Screenshots are at their real pixel size. A third person adds exactly one tile and one gap — the arithmetic is unit-tested.
04 · Shared screen

A window onto the screen, not a picture of it

The viewer is a normal tile. It shows the remote screen at a readable scale — about one remote pixel per physical pixel — cropped to whatever shape the tile has, and pans smoothly to keep the sharer’s cursor in view. Split it, resize it, move it to another workspace: the shape no longer matters.

Following without wobble

  • The sharer’s cursor can move anywhere in the middle 60% of the view without panning, so text stays still while you read.
  • It opens straight onto the sharer’s cursor instead of easing in from the middle.
  • Drag or scroll to look elsewhere; following pauses for a few seconds. Ctrl+scroll zooms.

Control through the crop

  • Clicks are mapped through the current pan and zoom back to the sharer’s screen; anything outside the visible image is never sent.
  • Follow, 1:1 and Fit in a pill that fades after a couple of idle seconds — no toolbar row.
  • The minimap shows where you are; click it to jump. It only appears when something is cropped.
05 · Peek

The whole screen, one key away

Super+H opens the remote screen fitted, in Hopp’s own scratchpad workspace, over whatever you’re doing. That’s monitor to monitor, so fitting the whole screen makes sense there. Press it again, or Esc, to drop back to your tiles.

06 · Overlays

Calls and sharing, without taking a tile

Call card

Incoming and outgoing calls appear top-right, where Omarchy notifications do. It floats above your tiles as a layer-shell surface and only takes the keyboard if you click it — a call never steals what you’re typing. ⏎ accepts, Esc declines.

Sharing pill

While you share, a pill under the bar says which monitor, who has control, and offers Revoke and Stop. Your viewers never see it: a Hyprland layer rule excludes both overlays from screen sharing.

08 · Hyprland rules

A few lines of Lua do the fitting

make install writes ~/.config/hypr/hopp.lua and one marked require line in hyprland.lua; make uninstall removes both. Same o.window and hl.layer_rule forms as Omarchy’s own files.

-- Call window: small, floating, on every workspace, never in your screen share.
o.window("hopp-omarchy.faces", {
  float = true, pin = true, no_initial_focus = true, no_screen_share = true,
  move = { "(monitor_w-window_w-10)", "(monitor_h-window_h-10)" },
})
-- The shared screen tiles normally. Peek gets its own scratchpad.
o.window("hopp-omarchy.peek", { workspace = "special:hopp" })
-- Overlays are for you, not your viewers.
hl.layer_rule({ match = { namespace = "^hopp-(call|sharing)$" }, no_screen_share = true })

o.bind("SUPER + ALT + H", "Hopp", hopp .. " --panel")
o.bind("SUPER + H", "Hopp: peek", hopp .. " --peek")
o.bind("SUPER + CTRL + ESCAPE", "Hopp: revoke control", hopp .. " --revoke-control")
09 · Status

Where it stands

The screenshots on this page are real native GPUI renders captured in an isolated headless compositor with synthetic users, cameras and screen content. They show the interface, not a live call.

Verified
  • 58 unit tests and strict Clippy, including viewport crop, follow and click-mapping maths and call-window sizing.
  • Every surface renders natively in the current Omarchy theme (14 scenes).
  • A real call between two accounts on the local Hopp backend, placed, accepted and left through the bar plugin’s bridge.
  • Hyprland 0.56 accepts the rules and bindings; the bar panel renders in the Omarchy shell.
  • Unchanged media path: H.264 screen video, Opus audio, camera tracks, scoped virtual input.
Still to see live
  • A screen share between two clients with the new viewer (the chooser appeared in a first try; no share was observed).
  • Following the sharer’s cursor, peek, and the sharing pill during a real share.
  • The bar panel during a live call.
  • Clipboard round trips, Mac/Windows interoperability, long calls and poor networks.
