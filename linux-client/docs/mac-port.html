Hopp Mac Port Guide

Everything built in the Linux client, explained so the Mac app can have it too: what each feature does, how it looks, the exact rules and numbers, where the Mac code should change, and how an agent proves it works.

Written from the code at fce1cb1 on useforesight/hopp main3 November 2026All names in examples are made up

Read the code, not the older Linux docs. Parts of linux-client/README.md, HANDOVER.md and docs/capture-transcripts.html still describe whisper.cpp, hopp-whisper and schema 2. The code now uses Parakeet, hopp-transcribe and schema 3. Every number in this guide was checked against the source.

Most of it is portable Rust. Capture, frames, the timeline, colours, sprites and cursor smoothing have no Linux dependency. They can move into a shared crate that both linux-client and core use. The Linux-only parts are the compositor plumbing, and the Mac app already has its own versions of most of those.

Tiling-desktop features are left out. Viewport tiles, Peek's special workspace, the bar plugin and the Hyprland rules don't apply to macOS. What not to port lists them.

Marks · phase 1

Cursors and colours

Each remote person gets a compact arrow in their colour, with a white edge and a small name tag tucked under it. While they draw it becomes a slim pencil, sitting exactly on the tip of their ink. This "Crisp" design was chosen from six prototypes on 3 November.

Linux · badge.rs, marks.rs

The rules

  • Arrow: 20×24 units, path M3 2.5 L3 20.5 L8 15.6 L15.6 14.8 Z, white 1.6 stroke. The tip (3, 2.5) is the pointer.
  • Pencil: 22×22 units; the lead (2.6, 19.4) is the pointer.
  • Tag: height 20, radius 5, 11 px semibold, 7 px side padding, at most 180 px with "…". Offset (+10, +14.5) from the arrow tip, (+12.4, −17.4) from the pencil.
  • Shadow: two layers, (0, 1.5, σ 1.4, 32%) and (0, 4, σ 4, 14%).
  • Text colour: WCAG luminance above 0.5 gives #14151a; anything else gives white.
  • Timing: name hides after 2.5 s over 350 ms, sliding 3 px. Cursor fades after 8 s over 400 ms. The name never hides while the pencil shows.
macOS · core/src/utils/svg_renderer.rs

How to build it

  • The Mac already renders cursor SVGs with resvg, the same crate. Replace the three templates in svg_renderer.rs:129-330 with the Crisp arrow, pencil and tag. Keep the hand for remote control.
  • Hide timings differ: Mac hides the whole cursor after 5 s (participant.rs:14). Use 2.5 s for the name and 8 s for the cursor.
  • Draw the tag as its own sprite. It has to fade and slide separately from the arrow.
  • The sharer's own overlay (window_manager.rs) and the viewer canvas (drawing_helpers::ParticipantOverlay) both need the change.

Cursor playback smoothing

Cursors surged when every network update started a new glide. Linux now plays each cursor back slightly behind the network, at an even speed (marks.rs:112-262). Port this into core/src/graphics/cursor.rs.

  • Lag: starts at 35 ms. Its target is max(gap × 2.2, slow × 1.3), clamped to 16–90 ms.
  • Lag changes slowly: playback runs at most 10% slow while the lag grows, and 3% fast while it shrinks.
  • Gap estimate: an exponential moving average (factor 0.05) of arrival gaps, clamped to 4–50 ms.
  • Reset: after 250 ms with no update, playback restarts from where the cursor rests.
  • Follows the pen: drawing sends ink points, not cursor moves, so the cursor follows the ink tip.
  • Test to port: a_pointer_moves_evenly_through_uneven_updates. Updates every 14–18 ms arrive 0–20 ms late, painted at 120 Hz. The mean step must be within 10% of expected, and the worst step within 30%.

One colour per person, on every machine

The Mac gives colours in join order, so one person can be blue on your Mac and pink on mine. Linux takes the colour from a hash of the Hopp user id. Everyone in the call then agrees, and the colour matches the captured frames and transcript.

  • Palette (ink.rs:10-19): eight colours, shown below.
  • Preferred colour: FNV-1a 64-bit of the user id, modulo 8. The user id is the third part of room:<room>:<user>:audio.
  • Clashes: if the colour is taken, take the next free one that isn't a look-alike. Look-alikes are blue and cyan, red and pink, and orange and yellow.
  • Order: you first, then everyone else in the order they first appear.

Stamps

A stamp is a numbered pin with words attached. You press Stamp, click a spot on the screen and type. Everyone in the call sees the marker drop in with a small bounce, pulse while it takes words, and show a light card with your name and text. It came from Scrawl's numbered pins.

Linux · session.rs, marks.rs

The rules

  • Marker: path M0 0 L-5 -8 A 11 11 0 1 1 5 -8 Z from the foot. The head centre is 17.8 above the foot, radius 11. Number bold 11.5 px (9 px for three digits).
  • Drop: 460 ms, ease-out-back, from 22 px above and 62% size. A ground shadow widens as it lands.
  • Listening: two rings breathe out from the head every 1.6 s, half a period apart.
  • Card: white at 98%, radius 8, padding 11×8, at most 290 px wide and 6 lines. The name is the placer's colour mixed 22% towards #1c1d26. Words still being recognised show at 45%.
  • Placement: right of the head if there's room, otherwise left; top edge 22 px above the head; kept 6 px inside the screen.
  • Removal: fades over 260 ms. Text is capped at 2,000 bytes.

How to build it

  • Viewer: add Stamp to the segmented control in screensharing_window.rs:323-339 (Remote control, Draw, Point). Stamp and Draw turn each other off.

Marks · nothing to build

Keeping marks out of the share

Viewers draw every cursor and stroke themselves, from data messages. If the sharer's overlay also went into the video, viewers would see every mark twice. So the overlay must be on the sharer's screen and absent from what they share.

On Linux this took a Hyprland plugin. Hyprland can't leave a surface out of a share; it paints a black box in its place. The marks overlay covers the whole monitor, so the first real call showed viewers a black screen. On macOS the window server does it for you.

Capture · phase 3

How Capture works

Capture writes a transcript of every call, on your own machine, with the right speaker on every line. Nothing is uploaded. Each person's audio is transcribed separately, so there's no speaker guessing. Joins, shares, control changes, bookmarks and screen frames are noted between the lines.

Every call is captured from the moment it connects. A capture stopped by hand stays stopped for the rest of that call. "auto": false in capture.json turns this off.

Linux · media.rs, capture/mod.rs

Where audio is tapped

  • Remote people: in each track's own decode task, before mixing (media.rs:1060-1078), keyed by participant identity.
  • You: after WebRTC echo cancellation, which is fed the real speaker output (media.rs:536). Speaker bleed is therefore never put on your line.
  • Never blocks the call: one atomic load per 10 ms frame when idle; try_lock and try_send when capturing.
  • Queue: 600 frames, about 2 s for three people. Overflow is counted and written as a gap line if 100 ms or more was lost.
macOS · core/src/livekit/audio.rs

Where to tap on the Mac

  • Remote people: every remote track already has its own NativeAudioStream. Tap in play_remote_audio_track, before push_samples (audio.rs:205-206).
  • You: in audio/capturer.rs, after echo cancellation. The mixer already feeds echo cancellation's reverse stream (mixer.rs:188-200).
  • Sample rate: the Mac streams are 16 kHz mono; Linux taps 48 kHz. Voice detection counts 10 ms frames, so it works at 16 kHz with 160-sample frames. Skip the resampler.
  • The recorder belongs in hopp_core, where LiveKit lives. The Tauri app only reads the store.

Parakeet and the helper

Capture moved from whisper.cpp to Parakeet on 2 November. It makes fewer errors and runs several times faster. The model runs in a separate process, hopp-transcribe, so a crash or a stuck GPU never takes the call down.

Linux · transcribe/, capture/helper.rs, models.rs

The helper

  • Engine: transcribe-cpp = "=0.2.4" with the vulkan feature, the same version as the Hex voice app.
  • Protocol: stdin takes a big-endian u32 header length, a JSON header {"id","samples"}, then f32 16 kHz samples. stdout sends one ready line, then one JSON line per request with word segments.
  • Limits: at most 30 s per request. Audio under 1.1 s is padded with silence, because Parakeet mishears very short clips.
  • One session for every request: creating one costs about 220 ms on the GPU.
  • Models: parakeet-en (default), parakeet-v3 (25 European languages), and Whisper GGUFs from tiny to large-v3. All are pinned to a revision and a SHA-256.
  • Reuses Hex's copy when Hex's receipt matches the pinned hash, size and modified time, so there's no second download.
macOS

How to build it

  • Build with Metal. metal is transcribe-cpp's default feature, so drop the vulkan feature for macOS builds.
  • Bundle the helper in Hopp.app/Contents/MacOS/ beside hopp_core. Sign and notarise it with the app.
  • No cgroups on macOS. Linux caps memory and gives the helper idle CPU through a systemd scope. On the Mac, start it with background QoS (taskpolicy -b or setpriority), and watch its footprint instead of capping it.
  • Models: store them in ~/Library/Application Support/com.hopp.app/models/ (suggested path).
  • Check the desktop stays smooth. On Apple silicon under Linux, GPU inference stalled the compositor until a kernel fix. macOS schedules the GPU differently, but measure frame pacing anyway.

Transcript rules

Each rule below fixed something seen on a real call. Port them with their tests; most are small and easy to lose.

Cutting speech into utterances

Rule (vad.rs:10-36)ValueWhy
SpeechLevel above max(noise floor + 12 dB, −50 dBFS); floor is the quietest of the last 2 sAdapts to each person's room
Kept before speech200 msKeeps first consonants
Silence that ends an utterance700 msWithin the usual 300–800 ms
Shortest utterance250 msDrops clicks and coughs
After 8 sA 200 ms pause ends it15 of 185 lines on one call ended mid-sentence at the hard cut
Hard cut20 s, at the quietest moment of the last 2 sThe rest carries on as the next utterance
Speaker goes idle1.5 sFlushes their last words

Sending, splitting and cleaning

  • Parakeet sends each utterance as soon as the helper is free, about 0.7 s after the speaker pauses. Whisper waits for a fuller request (24 s, or 12 s of waiting), one person per request, because it only gives segment times.
  • Utterances share a request: 1 s of silence between one person's utterances, 3 s between people, at most 29 s.
  • Words go back to their utterance by start time. Parakeet stretches the last word before a pause across the silence after it. Placing words by their middle gave them to the next utterance. Each word goes to the utterance whose span, widened by 500 ms, contains it.
  • Finished speech always goes before live drafts in the helper's queue.
  • Cleaning (text.rs): strip [...], (...), *...* and ♪. Drop stock phrases such as "thank you" and "bye" only when the utterance is under 1.5 s, since models invent them from noise.

How lines are shown

  • One person's lines join into a message when nothing comes between them and the pause is at most 5 s.
  • Reactions don't split someone else's turn. A reaction is at most 3 words and 1.5 s, such as "Yeah" or "Hmm". It's shown after the message it interrupted. On one 17-minute call this took 113 messages down to 85. The stored lines are unchanged.
  • One voice on two mics shows once. Lines from two people within 3 s, with Dice-bigram similarity of 0.6 or more and a length ratio of 0.55 or more, are merged. The first speaker is kept, with "also on Alex's mic, shown once".
  • Wake phrases match known mishearings. Parakeet heard "hey Claude" as "Heckord" and "Hey Cord". "hey claude" also wakes on heckord, hey cord, hey clod, hey cloud, hey claud and hey clawed. Wake phrases match final text only.
Capture · phase 4

Live text

A line appears as a dimmed draft when someone starts speaking, grows as words settle, and turns final under the same line id when they pause. Studies of live captions found that flicker tires people more than errors do. So a word, once shown, is never taken back.

Press Play. The demo runs the real agreement rule from draft.rs on made-up guesses. It shows a word only when two guesses in a row agree on it, or when it's more than 1 s behind the live edge.

Linux · draft.rs, recorder.rs

The rules

  • Guesses: the utterance so far is re-transcribed at 1.0 s, then every 600 ms until 2 s, then every 1.5 s.
  • A guess only extends what's shown if its start matches the shown text.
  • Final text replaces the draft in place, or the line becomes retracted if nothing was said.
  • Needs a word-timed model (Parakeet) on the GPU, with "Live text" on.
  • Measured on Linux: first words a median 2.1 s after speech began; final text 0.56 s after the pause; transcriber busy for 26% of the audio. Each pass costs about 0.23 s of GPU time whatever its length.
macOS

Notes for the port

  • It comes with the recorder; there's nothing Mac-specific in it.
  • Why not streaming: transcribe.cpp allows one stream per loaded model, with no other runs while it's open.
  • Measure on the Mac before trusting the Linux figures. No script for them is committed yet; Performance checks describes one.

Safeguards, each from a real failure

  • Never reopen the database to set permissions. Closing any descriptor of a file drops all the process's POSIX locks on it, SQLite's included. A reader then deleted the live WAL and two calls lost their lines. macOS has the same lock rules. Create the file only when it doesn't exist.
  • A lock on <id>.live marks a recorder as alive. A "capturing" call nobody holds for 30 s is closed with "ended unexpectedly".
  • A stuck request restarts the helper: after 60 s plus 4× its audio on the GPU, or 120 s plus 10× on the CPU.
  • Failure ladder: restart once, then fall back from GPU to CPU. Retry after 30 s, doubling to 2 minutes. A capture never gives up while running.
  • Backlog over 10 minutes is skipped with a gap line, so the transcript catches up.
  • Leaving the call never waits for the transcript to finish.
Capture · phase 5 · from Scrawl

Screen frames

The moments that matter become numbered stills of the shared screen, with everyone's ink painted in. They appear in the transcript as lines such as [00:32] Frame #6 Sam circled something at middle left (12%, 53%). Agents see them as images. The idea comes from Kit Langton's Scrawl, a tool for showing a coding agent a problem.

Stills were chosen over video. Video would be smaller, but needs a decoder in the window and stills for agents anyway. Tuple also keeps stills taken when the screen changes.

Linux

Detection and files

  • Scrawl's measure: a 64×36 luma thumbnail. The difference is the larger of the mean change and 1.4× the fourth most changed block of an 8×6 grid, against 0.08. A moving pointer doesn't count.
  • Plus a cell count: 6 or more cells changed by over 0.025 also count. Scrawl's measure alone missed one-line code edits, often the change that matters in pairing.
  • Why so strict: one sample used to be enough. A 5-minute call took 68 frames, mostly while scrolling; under these rules it takes about 11.
  • Files: .full.webp at full size, .webp at most 1440 px for agents, and .json with each mark. WebP quality 85, about 0.3 MB a frame, roughly 0.1 GB an hour.
  • Captions name the shape: circled, underlined, drew a line, dropped pin 3, stamped pin 4 with its words, bookmarked. Shapes are worked out from the stroke, not sent.
macOS

Where frames come from

  • Your own share: the ScreenCaptureKit frames in core/src/capture/macos_stream.rs, before they go to the NativeVideoSource. Downscale to a short side of 1440 first, as Linux does.
  • Someone else's share: the decoded frames the viewer window gets, before yuv_renderer.rs.
  • Ink is painted in software (ink_raster.rs, tiny-skia), so frames look the same whatever the GPU.
  • Capture hides your cursor (with_shows_cursor(false)), so frames show only the marks.
  • All of the frame logic is portable, using the image and webp crates.

Grouping rules

  • Drawings: frames within 3 s of each other and 10 s in total form one card, shown as its last frame with each mark listed once.
  • Crop: around the marks with 5% padding, at least 34% wide and 30% tall, grown towards 16:9 and kept inside the frame.
  • Screen bursts: frames within 8 s of each other and 40 s in total, with up to 6 evenly spaced thumbnails, always including the last.
  • Same second: events, then bookmarks, then screens, then drawings, then speech.
  • Chips per person and kind of mark ("Lee · pins 1, 2") outline those marks on hover.
Linux · captures.rs

Window behaviour

  • Size: 1180×820, minimum 360×320. Below 760 px the call list folds behind a button.
  • Scrubber: one lane per speaker, screen changes as bars, drawings as dots; click to jump.
  • Live: polls every 600 ms by rev, follows the tail, and updates drafts in place. A new capture is selected unless you're reading another live call.
  • Long calls: a virtual list draws only visible rows, and at most 36 decoded frames are held.
  • Viewer: any picture opens full size; arrow keys step and Esc closes.
  • Header actions: Bookmark and Stop while live; copy an agent prompt; Open Markdown and Delete (click twice) once ended.

Wire protocol

Linux speaks Hopp's existing protocol unchanged, and adds one topic and one attribute. Coordinates everywhere are fractions of the shared display, 0 to 1, from the top left. Older apps ignore what they don't understand: core logs a parse error and drops the packet.

{"type":"StampPlace","payload":{"id":3,"x":0.62,"y":0.41}}

{"type":"StampRemove","payload":{"id":3}}
  • A stamp is identified by (sender, id). Ids count up per sender.
  • Every keystroke resends the whole text with done: false. draft is for words still being recognised, reserved for spoken stamps; Linux always sends it empty today.
  • Validation: the point must be within 0–1, and text plus draft at most 2,000 bytes. Packets over 512 KiB, or from someone not in the call, are dropped.
  • Numbers aren't sent. Each client numbers stamps in the order it saw them, so two machines can disagree. Decide whether to fix this before both clients ship stamps widely.
  • Linux sends participant_location unreliably and the rest reliably. The Mac sends everything reliably. Both work.

What the scripted call asserts

  1. Speech lines come from exactly two speakers.
  2. Every mark is on a frame, within 0.01 of where it was drawn.
  3. A frame exists for the bookmark.
  4. Every stamp is on a frame with its exact text and position.

Four runs on Linux found real bugs: two people given the same colour, a slide change dropped soon after a frame, an MCP error once the call ended, and one-line code edits that didn't count as screen changes. In every run the agent's summary of the bug and fix was right.

Lessons that carry over

Closing a file drops its locks

Any close of any descriptor drops the process's POSIX locks on that file. It cost two calls their lines. Same on macOS.

Place words by start time

Parakeet stretches the last word over the pause after it. Placing by the middle moved words to the wrong person.

Test two tracks on one timeline

Batched words can move to the wrong person. capture transcribe Alex=a.wav Sam=b.wav catches it.

Separate harness from product failures

A failed harness run doesn't prove a product bug. Label evidence as synthetic, harness or real.

Measure latency end to end

The video lag was the receiver's jitter buffer, not the encoder. Tuning the wrong end would have wasted the work.

GPU memory belongs to whoever allocates it

Set memory limits from what the system charges the process, not from RSS.

Don't put marks in the video

An earlier fix painted marks into the share for Mac viewers. Their apps already draw marks, so they saw everything twice. It was reverted within the hour.

Check before heavy work

Builds and benchmarks during a live call starved its capture, and the rest of that call's speech was lost.

Open issues on Linux

Don't copy these gaps. Some features were checked in harnesses but not yet on a real call.

  • Not yet seen on a real call: marks over your own share through the plugin, drawing on your own screen, stamps between two real clients, and WebP frames.
  • Stamps are hard to find. They're only in the viewer, so they don't exist while you share. The Mac pill can fix this.
  • Stamp numbers can differ between machines and frames. Captured frames number stamps when finished, and count the stamp's ripple as a pin. This is a reading of the code, not yet seen.
  • Possible bug: end_call in session.rs doesn't clear stamps or the active stamp.
  • No auto-capture switch in Settings; only the JSON file.
  • Interoperability with Mac and Windows, long calls and poor networks are untested.
  • Not built yet: spoken stamps from the placer's live text, spoken cues such as "look here" as frame triggers, a screen-at-a-time command for agents, and app and window names saved with each frame, as Tuple does.
